NLO TAD Guide

Puts the patient's intraoral scan onto their CBCT, finds and checks TAD sites between the roots, and makes a printable tooth-borne surgical guide. Runs on an office PC - the CBCT and the scan never leave the office, and no AI service is involved.

Everyday use about 5 minutes

1

Export the CBCT from CS 3D Imaging: open the volume, click Export Volume (the skull with an arrow), pick a folder, matrix size and slice thickness Same as source, CT (one file per slice), Uncompressed, Create DICOMDir off → Export. Zip the folder if you like. Then export the patient's scan of the arch the TADs go in - STL or PLY, from the AlliedStar or the iTero.

2

Open TAD Guide (home page → TAD Guide). Drop the CBCT folder (or its zip) and the scan on the page together, check the arch, Start. For a very big export on the office share, paste the folder's path instead - it is read where it is, not copied.

3

Match. The app puts the scan on the CBCT by itself (10-30 seconds). Check it: the scan's teeth are coloured by fit (green within 0.2 mm), and in the three slices under the 3D view the coral line (the scan) hugs the CBCT crowns - shift-click the model to look anywhere. Looks right to go on. Off? Match again, nudge it under Fine adjust, or Match by 3 points: click a cusp tip on the scan, then the same spot on the CBCT teeth, three times (a molar each side and an incisor).

4

TADs. Pick the site - buccal between roots, palatal between roots, infrazygomatic crest, buccal shelf or paramedian palate - and Find sites: every gap between the roots is checked and ranked, with root clearance, cortical bone and soft tissue for each. Use the one you want, or Place by clicking on the gum. Click a placed TAD to see three cross-sections along and across it - the red tint is what the app counted as root. Move it mesial / distal, up / down, change the tilt, depth or which TAD - the list is ours: tomas-pin SD / EP 06-10 and the Great Lakes MSE TADs 1.8 × 9-13; Fine-tune here looks for the best spot within 1.5 mm and 8°.

5

Guide. Make the guide (10-30 seconds). Download the Guide STL (it lines up with the original scan file), the TADs STL to check it against, and Plan / print for the chart (it shows the driver stop: how far the sleeve top is above the seated TAD head). Open in PreForm on the PC that runs the app. Print in Formlabs Surgical Guide resin, not the model resin.

6

Save & finish. The case teaches the app. After the try-in, tap how it fitted - tight / good / loose on the teeth and for the driver in the sleeve - and the next guides adjust.

What it checks

From your TAD course notes:

Root clearanceunder 0.5 mm: stop · under 1 mm: caution · 1.2-1.6 mm is the safe range in the literature
Cortical bonemeasured along the screw. Under 0.5 mm: do not insert · 0.5-1.5: no pre-drill · 1.5-2.5: perforate the cortex · over 2.5: perforate + pilot ~3 mm
Soft tissueover 3 mm: fine not to seat the collar all the way (or a longer collar)
Bonehow much of the thread is in bone; sinus / nasal floor entry - on the palate, reaching the nasal floor counts as bicortical
Lower buccalno TADs at L5/6 or L6/7 (bone too thick) - flagged; check the mandibular canal on the slices

Roots, cortex and sinus are estimated from the CBCT's grey values: dentin that stands out from what surrounds it, deeper than the cortical plate. It leans toward calling something a root. The cross-sections are what to trust - they show exactly what the numbers are based on.

How it learns numbers only

  • Matching: each accepted match is remembered per scanner and arch, so the next scan from the same scanner starts from that pose and usually matches on the first try.
  • Your habits: per site, the depth, tilt and screw size of the TADs you place or adjust by hand become where the site finder starts (a suggestion used as it came doesn't count - that would only teach it its own choices).
  • Guide fit: each tight on the teeth adds 0.03 mm to the fit gap, each loose takes 0.02 off; for the driver in the sleeve, 0.10 / 0.05 mm on the hole - the guides settle on your printer and resin.
  • CBCT levels: if you move the bone / teeth levels by hand, it keeps how far you moved them for that CBCT machine.

Lessons are small files in the app's training folder: numbers only - no names, no images, no scans. Settings → What it has learned lists them.

Patient data

  • Everything runs on the office PC that hosts the app. The CBCT, the scan and the guide are kept in the app's jobs folder and deleted 30 days after the case was last worked on (Settings to change). Delete case removes them at once.
  • The app answers this PC and the office network only - no address on the internet, no cloud, no AI service. From home, use Jump Desktop into an office PC.

One-time setup on the PC that runs it about 10 minutes, once

1

Get the app folder onto the PC - the one that runs Tray Maker is a good home (Python is already there): download the nlo-apps zip (green Code button → Download ZIP), right-click → Extract All, and put the nlo-tadguide folder somewhere permanent, for example C:\NLO\nlo-tadguide.

2

Double-click install.bat in it. It finds Python (already there on a PC that runs Tray Maker; otherwise it downloads the installer - run it, Install Now, then install.bat again), installs the components, and asks whether to start the app at sign-in - type y.

3

Double-click start_app.bat once to start it now. If Windows asks about the firewall, allow private networks. The small black window "NLO TAD Guide" must stay open; other office PCs reach it at http://NAME:8767.

Updating: in the app, Settings → Check for updates → Update now (from any office PC; the lab PC restarts the app in about a minute). Or double-click update.bat in the app folder, or press Win + R and run:
powershell -NoExit -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -Command "[Net.ServicePointManager]::SecurityProtocol='Tls12'; iex (iwr 'https://raw.githubusercontent.com/amooloo/nlo-apps/main/nlo-tadguide/update.ps1' -UseBasicParsing).Content" Settings, cases and lessons are kept.
