NLO Virtual Tech

Aligner setups to your own axes. The scans are split into teeth, every tooth gets a proposed tooth line and long axis, you correct them, and the setup is worked out from them - then back to the axes and again, as often as you like. Runs on an office PC: the scans never leave the office, and no AI service is involved.

Everyday use about 5 minutes

1

New case. Export the upper and lower scans from AS Connect (PLY keeps the colour, which helps find the gum line), open Virtual Tech (home page → Virtual Tech), type the name, drop both scans, Start. The teeth are split and every tooth's axes proposed in a minute or two.

2

Axes. The orange teeth are the ones to check. Click a tooth: from above, the blue dot turns the tooth line (fissure on back teeth, incisal edge on front teeth) and the green ball slides it; face-on the red dot sets tip, from the side torque. Save my corrections.

3

Run lower setup (about 2 minutes; it saves your axes first). The two boxes under it are the limits for making room: first molars out (mm a side, standing in for the WALA limit) and incisors forward (mm). Room is made in your order: expansion, incisors forward, then IPR - back contacts first, then front, 0.8 mm at most, none distal to the 6s.

4

Setup. Start / Play / Finish, the IPR at the finish and the plan tooth by tooth. Back to the axes to change anything and run it again; Run again with these tries other limits. Every saved version of your axes is kept.

How the setup is made

  • Every tooth is lined up by your tooth line and crossing point on one smooth arch, set to Andrews' tip and torque (torque held to 12° of change per tooth), the contacts closed and the marginal ridges evened, with a 0.5 mm reverse curve at the premolars.
  • The second molars move as little as possible. IPR shows only from the IPR stage on, as stripping that keeps the contact rounded.
  • The gum is the scanned gum: about 2 mm past each tooth's gum line moves with the tooth and blends into the rest.
  • The upper setup is a draft for now: it is lined up on its own and not yet fitted to the lower.

How it learns your axes only

Every time you run a setup, the teeth you changed or checked are kept in the app's training folder (each tooth's crown shape with your line and axis - no names). The next case's proposed axes are carried over from the teeth most like it, so the first guesses get closer to how you set them.

Patient data

  • Everything runs on the office PC that hosts the app. Scans, axes and setups are kept in the app's cases folder; Delete on the case list moves a case to cases\_deleted.
  • The app answers this PC and the office network only - no address on the internet, no cloud, no AI service. From home, use Jump Desktop into an office PC.

On the PC that runs it once

1

The app is in C:\NLO\nlo-virtualtech on the lab PC. Double-click install.bat there (it uses the same Python as Tray Maker and TAD Guide) and type y to start the app at sign-in.

2

Double-click start_app.bat once to start it now. If Windows asks about the firewall, allow private networks. The small black window "NLO Virtual Tech" must stay open; other office PCs reach it at http://lab:8768.

After new files are put in the folder, double-click restart_app.bat. Cases and the training folder are kept.
